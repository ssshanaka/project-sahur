## Lesson 3.6 — Top-k Sampling

In **Lesson 3.5**, we learned how **Temperature ($T$)** scales raw logits to reshape the Softmax probability curve. We saw that $T = 0.8$ provides a great balance for Sahur's Gen Z persona.

However, Temperature scaling alone leaves a hidden vulnerability: **the long-tail distribution problem**.

Qwen3-0.6B has a massive vocabulary of **151,646 tokens**. Even if a Temperature of $0.8$ suppresses improbable tokens, a weird or completely irrelevant token at index #120,000 might still retain a tiny $0.0001\%$ chance of being sampled. In a multi-step autoregressive loop generating hundreds of tokens, that tiny roll of the dice will eventually trigger a catastrophic hallucination or gibberish output.

In this lesson, we will master **Top-k Sampling**—a hard truncation filter that chops off the bottom $151,646 - k$ candidate tokens before sampling even begins!

# Step 1 — Why does this even exist?

Imagine you ask Sahur to complete this sentence:

> `"The capital of France is ____"`

Qwen3 computes probabilities for all 151,646 tokens in its vocabulary:

- Token `"Paris"`: $88.0\%$
- Token `"a"`: $4.0\%$
- Token `"located"`: $2.0\%$
- ...
- Token `"#89124 (a random Chinese character)"`: $0.00005\%$
- Token `"#142100 (a C++ code syntax symbol)"`: $0.00002\%$

If you run stochastic sampling across the entire 151,646 distribution, you are rolling a 151,646-sided die. Even if $99.9\%$ of the wheel is valid English words, $0.1\%$ is filled with thousands of bizarre tail tokens.

**Top-k Sampling solves this by asking one simple question:**

> _"Why keep 151,646 tokens in memory when only the top 50 make any actual sense?"_

By setting $k = 50$, we instantly banish the bottom 151,596 tokens from the selection wheel, guaranteeing that Sahur never picks a low-probability tail token.

---

# Step 2 — Internal Mechanics

Here is the exact 4-step algorithm PyTorch executes when you set `top_k = k` inside `model.generate()`:

```text
               151,646 Raw Logits from Model Output
                                │
                                ▼
1. SORT & TRUNCATE  ──► Rank logits high-to-low and select top-k (e.g. k=50).
                                │
                                ▼
2. MASK TAIL LOGITS ──► Set all remaining 151,596 logits to -Infinity (-∞).
                                │
                                ▼
3. SOFTMAX          ──► Calculate Softmax over top-k (probabilities sum to 1.0).
                                │
                                ▼
4. SAMPLE           ──► Sample 1 token from the truncated top-k distribution!

```

---

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Walkthrough:

Suppose our vocabulary had only 6 tokens, and after Temperature scaling, their raw logits were:

$$\text{Logits} = [ \mathbf{8.0}, \mathbf{6.0}, \mathbf{4.0}, 2.0, 1.0, -1.0 ]$$

If we set **$k = 3$**:

1. **Sort & Truncate**: We identify the top 3 highest logits: $[8.0, 6.0, 4.0]$.
2. **Masking**: We overwrite all logits outside the top 3 with negative infinity ($-\infty$):

$$\text{Masked Logits} = [ \mathbf{8.0}, \mathbf{6.0}, \mathbf{4.0}, -\infty, -\infty, -\infty ]$$

3. **Softmax Re-normalization**: When we calculate Softmax ($P_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum e^{z_j}}$):

- $e^{-\infty} = 0$.
- The probability of tokens 4, 5, and 6 becomes **exactly $0.00\%$**.
- The remaining top 3 tokens are re-normalized so their probabilities sum to **$100\%$**.

4. **Sampling**: `torch.multinomial` picks a token strictly from those top 3 candidates.

### 📦 Cell 1: Environment & Model Initialization

**What this cell does:** Loads `Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B` into 16-bit precision on your Colab T4 GPU VRAM.

import torch

    device_map="auto"

# Step 4 — Experiment: The Fatal Flaw of Fixed Top-k

Top-k is a massive improvement over raw sampling, but it has a fundamental flaw: **It uses a static $k$ regardless of model confidence.**

Let's compare two different scenarios:

#### Scenario A: High Confidence (Model knows the answer)

- Prompt: `"2 + 2 = ____"`
- Token `"4"` has a **$99.9\%$ probability**.
- Tokens 2 through 50 have near **$0\%$ probability**.
- **Top-k Problem ($k=50$)**: Keeping 50 tokens forces the model to keep 49 garbage options even though token `"4"` was overwhelmingly obvious!

#### Scenario B: Low Confidence (Model is being creative)

- Prompt: `"Once upon a time in a distant galaxy, there lived a ____"`
- There are **200 valid nouns** that could fit here naturally (e.g., `"alien"`, `"dragon"`, `"starfighter"`, `"wizard"`...).
- **Top-k Problem ($k=50$)**: Setting $k=50$ artificially chops off 150 perfectly good creative words!

# 🧠 Mental Model

Think of Top-k Sampling like a college basketball tryout:

- **Raw Vocabulary (151,646 tokens)** = 151,646 people show up to the gym, including people who have never held a basketball in their life.
- **Top-k ($k = 50$)** = The coach says: _"No matter who shows up today, I am making a cut after the first drill and keeping strictly the Top 50 players on the roster."_
- **The Good**: You successfully eliminate the bottom 151,596 players who can't play basketball.
- **The Flaw**: If 200 world-class players show up, you accidentally cut 150 great players. If only 5 good players show up, you are forced to keep 45 bad players just to meet your fixed quota of 50.

# 🛠 Mini Lab: PyTorch Math Verification

Let's build a raw PyTorch Top-k filter from scratch using `torch.topk()`!

Run this cell in Google Colab:

```python
import torch

# 1. Create simulated raw logits for 6 vocabulary tokens
logits = torch.tensor([12.0, 8.0, 5.0, 3.0, 1.0, -2.0])
tokens = ["Paris", "France", "Europe", "City", "Banana", "SyntaxError"]

print("--- ORIGINAL LOGITS & SOFTMAX ---")
orig_probs = torch.softmax(logits, dim=-1)
for t, l, p in zip(tokens, logits, orig_probs):
    print(f"Token: {t:<12} | Logit: {l:<5.1f} | Prob: {p*100:5.2f}%")

# 2. Execute Top-k Filtering (k = 3)
k = 3
top_values, top_indices = torch.topk(logits, k)

# Create a mask filled with -Infinity
filtered_logits = torch.full_like(logits, float('-inf'))

# Scatter top-k values back into the filtered tensor
filtered_logits.scatter_(0, top_indices, top_values)

# 3. Compute Re-normalized Softmax Probabilities
topk_probs = torch.softmax(filtered_logits, dim=-1)

print(f"\n--- AFTER TOP-K (k = {k}) TRUNCATION & RE-NORMALIZATION ---")
for t, l, p in zip(tokens, filtered_logits, topk_probs):
    print(f"Token: {t:<12} | Logit: {l:<5} | Prob: {p*100:5.2f}%")

```

**Observation:**
Notice how tokens outside the top 3 get a logit score of `-inf` and their Softmax probability drops to **$0.00\%$**, while `"Paris"`, `"France"`, and `"Europe"` are re-normalized so their probabilities sum to **$100.00\%$**!
